         1     EXT. COUNTY ROAD BUS SHELTER - NIGHT      1

               Rain on a tin roof. One bulb. RENNIE (40s), wet coat, a
               duffel she has packed too carefully, sits alone on the
               bench. She checks a paper ticket. Checks it again.

               Headlights. A pickup rolls in and idles. CAL (30s), her
               younger brother, gets out without killing the engine.

                                     CAL
                         You weren't going to call.

                                     RENNIE
                         I left a note.

                                     CAL
                         On the fridge. Under the magnet
                         from Mom's church. Where nobody
                         looks.

                                     RENNIE
                               (not looking at him)
                         Then why are you here?

                                     CAL
                         Because you always do this. Get in
                         the truck, Ren.

                                     RENNIE
                         I'm not angry, Cal. I'm just done.

               She tears the ticket in half. Then in half again. Lets the
               pieces fall into the rain.

                                     CAL
                         That looked angry.

                                     MOM (V.O.)
                               (on his phone,
                               speaker)
                         Calvin, did you find her? Is she
                         eating?

               Cal kills the call. Rennie almost laughs.


         2     INT. CAL'S PICKUP - MOVING - CONTINUOUS      2

               Wipers. The heater roars and does nothing. Rennie holds the
               duffel on her lap like it might leave without her.

                                     CAL
                         Where were you going to go?

                                     RENNIE
                         Does it matter?

                                     CAL
                         It matters to the guy who just
                         drove forty minutes in this.

                                     RENNIE
                         Tucson. There's a woman there who
                         rents rooms over a bakery. You wake
                         up and the whole place smells like
                         bread. I read about it. I read
                         about it for two years, Cal. Every
                         night after Dad's pills, after the
                         dishes, after you went home to your
                         actual life, I read about a room
                         over a bakery in Tucson and I
                         thought, one day. One day I'm going
                         to be a person who wakes up
                         somewhere that smells like bread.

                                     CAL
                         You never said anything.

                                     RENNIE
                         I'm saying it now.

                                                            CUT TO:


         3     EXT. TRUCK STOP MOTEL - PARKING LOT - DAWN      3

               Gray light. The pickup is parked crooked across two spaces.
               Rennie is outside it, duffel on her shoulder. Cal stands at
               the open driver's door.

                                     CAL
                         The house. Tell me you didn't.

                                     RENNIE
                         Signed it Thursday.

                                     CAL
                         That's Dad's house.

                                     RENNIE
                         Dad's been gone eleven months. It's
                         a house.

               Cal slams the truck door. Rennie doesn't flinch. She's been
               waiting for that sound.

                                     CAL
                         Get back in the truck.

                                     RENNIE
                         No.

                                     CAL
                         Ren, I swear to God.
                         I SAID GET IN THE TRUCK.

                                     RENNIE
                               (quiet)
                         Half of it's yours. It's already in
                         your account. I'm not running away
                         from you, Cal. I'm just not taking
                         care of anybody anymore.

               She walks toward the highway. Cal watches her go. He doesn't
               follow.
