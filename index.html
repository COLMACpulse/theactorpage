One studio, every step, every time.

The self-tape studio is built in, so every tape comes out the same way: framed, slated and clean. No juggling four apps to get one tape.

  • Framed the way the office asked: landscape or portrait, how close
  • Your slate, made for you, in the order they asked
  • Your reader plays by the lens, from the right line
  • After each take, what it heard: a line you went quiet on, a spot where the sound clipped. Not a grade

WRITERS BLOCKAGE screenwriting, by hand or out loud OPEN

A full screenplay editor. Type it, or write by talking on your phone.

Open Writer's Blockage
More

A full screenplay editor in the browser. On a phone, write by talking. Say "exterior bus shelter, night" and you get the heading. Say "Rennie says I left a note" and you get the cue and the line. "Scratch that", "change ticket to note" and "try again" fix it without touching the screen. It formats what you say. It never writes a word you didn't. At the desk: index cards, outline, cast, stats, draft compare, scene and character reports, global rename, focus mode. Exports a formatted PDF, Fountain, Final Draft .fdx, RTF and plain text.

PAPER TRAIL every draft, kept OPEN

Every draft kept and compared, then sent out.

Open Paper Trail
More

Every draft you have, never overwritten. Drop scripts and storyboards (PDF, Final Draft, Fountain, Word) or keep one straight from Writer's Blockage. Same title, same project. You pick the current draft. Each scene shows what changed since the last draft, line by line. Everything you ever cut lands in the Boneyard by itself, next to the bits you catch on the fly. Mark a setup and where it pays off, or a secret and who learns it; cut the payoff and it tells you. Hold a table read: readers tap each line, landed or lost me, and you see where they got lost and whether the next draft fixed it. Keep your research with it: photos, voice memos, video, PDFs and links, pinned to a scene or a character. Search all of it at once. Send a scene to Story Studio to shoot it. Package it (logline, synopsis, one-sheet, query) and keep a list of where it went.

STORY STUDIO crew or solo OPEN

Crew or solo: shot list, shoot day, takes, a pack for your editor. Backlot opens from it.

Open Story Studio
More

Shoot with a crew, or do it yourself. Your script comes off the shelf, split into scenes. For each one, pick how you make it: play every part, keep one face and turn the others into a shoulder, hands and a voice, or the fastest proof. You get the shot list, matched to the places, props and gear you own, a continuity card so the next scene matches (MATCH CAMERA lays the last photo over the live camera so you line it up by eye, with a self-timer for when you're the one in frame), and a shoot day grouped by place, light and wardrobe, with sunrise and sunset for each place, and a printable call sheet made from it. After the shoot, find any take by what you remember (bus shelter, last week, the laugh), check your footage folder against the takes you logged, cut to length, watch the cut and tap NOTE to pin a note to the take on screen, make it into a rough cut video right on the phone, re-record a line over picture (ADR) and drop it in, and hand your editor an edit list, end credits and a plain brief: what must stay, which take, what's missing, when to ask you. Phone and tablet.

Before you ask

What happens to my material if this company disappears?

Nothing. It's already on your phone or computer, not ours. On This Device backs up every tool to one file you keep, and restores it anywhere. The tools run without a server, so the copy you have keeps working.

Do you upload my self-tapes?

No. Nothing leaves your phone unless you send it: a self-tape you share, a page you publish, a link you send a friend to read in. Even then, only what you picked. The privacy page lists every time anything can go online.

Can I use it on more than one computer?

Yes. Back up on one with On This Device and restore on the other. It adds what's missing and never removes anything. Paid will sync it for you.

Who is building this?

A working actor, writer and director, for his own use first. Every tool here came out of a job that needed doing on a real set or in a real audition, which is why none of them try to tell you whether the take was any good.
