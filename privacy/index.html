Privacy

Your things stay with you

What the site does today. Last changed 20 Oct 2026.

There's no account, no sign-in, no tracking, no ads and no analytics. What you put in (your sides, scripts, takes, headshots, résumé, notes, voice) is kept in this browser, on this device. We don't have a copy (except a page you choose to put online), and nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it.

Where your things are kept

In this browser's own storage on this device. Each tool keeps its part, and the tools read each other on the same device, so you drop a thing once and every tool has it. Another phone or computer doesn't see it.

The pages themselves come from GitHub Pages, which hosts the site. Like any web host, GitHub sees that a page was asked for (an address and a time). It never sees what you put in. Their terms are in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

No cookies are set, and the pages load nothing from anyone else: the fonts and code are all served from this site.

The few times something can leave

Only when you do it:

  • You send it. A note to your reps or a pitch opens in your own mail app, or your share sheet, for you to send. A take, a page or a backup you save is a file on your device, and you choose where it goes.
  • You speak to a tool that types for you. Ten places can turn speech into words: Actor OS's MATCHING WORDS, MEMORIZER's SAY IT, NOTES, Paper Trail's SAY IT, BREAKDOWN's SAY IT (a note in your words), THEACTORPAGES's voice and its GO, the Hub's Speak the audition, GSD's mic, and WORDS on a video or voice note (FILM HUB and Paper Trail), which is off until you turn it on. That uses the browser's own speech service: Chrome sends what you say to Google, Safari to Apple. Each one says so where you use it. Actor OS's LISTENING (hearing when your line ends) stays on the device, and it's the one that's on by default.
  • You put your page online. In the Project Hub, PUT IT ONLINE sends that one page, with what you picked for it (headshots, videos, credits, your contact block if you leave it on), to our host on Cloudflare, at an address you choose. Anyone with the address can see it. Nothing else from your device goes. TAKE IT DOWN deletes it from the host. The key that can change your page stays on your device, with a backup code you keep; the host keeps only a scrambled copy of each. Like any web host, Cloudflare sees that a page was asked for (an address and a time).
  • You check THE WIRE. CHECK THE NEWS on your postcard goes online only when you tap it. A bar at the top says ONLINE while it does. It downloads one public file from GitHub, the same file for every phone: the California Film Commission's tax-credit list and recent headlines, links, tags and short summaries from Deadline, Variety and The Hollywood Reporter. Nothing about you is sent with it: no name, no types, no offices. What fits you is worked out on your device. Like any download, GitHub sees that the file was asked for (an address and a time). The file is made once an hour by a feed reader that names itself, reads only those four sites, and follows their robots rules.
  • You ask a friend to read in. The link you send has the scene's lines inside it, after the #, which browsers never send to a server. Anyone who has the link can read those lines, so send it only to someone you trust with the sides. Their recordings stay on their phone until they send you the file themselves.
  • The people in your work stay here. CONTACTS keeps names, emails and phone numbers on this device. They never go on a sheet you send, and the sheet for your reps never lists anyone's details. The SAG-AFTRA and talent-agent links on REPS open those sites, the way any link does.
  • Press sends nothing. PRESS keeps your campaigns, pitches and press contacts on this device. EMAIL IT opens your own mail app with your words in it; the press kit is a file you save and send yourself.
  • Your receipts and clips stay here. EXPENSES keeps receipt photos on this device until you save the file for your tax preparer. FOOTAGE trims your clip on this device and never downloads from a streaming site.
  • You look something up in the Hub. If you give the Hub your own TMDB key, the IMDb ID or title you look up is sent to The Movie Database to fetch credits and key art. Without a key, nothing is sent.
  • You open a link. A reel link, a trade story or a casting site opens where it goes, like any link.

Camera and mic

They only come on when you ask, and a bar at the top says so whenever they're on, and whether they're recording. They go off when you leave that step. What they record stays on the device until you send it.

Other people's details

Your reps, casting directors, cast and crew are kept the same way, on your device. Their phone numbers and emails never go on the pages the site makes for you to send (the list for your manager, your film page).

Keeping it, and losing it

  • Back up. On This Device makes one file of everything, every tool, that you keep. We never see it. Restoring adds what's missing and never removes.
  • The browser can clear it. Clearing this site's data in your browser settings deletes it all. Safari on an iPhone can clear a site's saved things after a stretch of not visiting; a site added to the Home Screen is kept. The site asks the browser to keep your things, and the browser decides.
  • Delete it yourself any time: remove it in the tool, or clear this site's data in the browser.
